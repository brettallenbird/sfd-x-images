For a long time, alcohol had a vote in everything. What I did after work. Where I went. How long I stayed. Whether I could be counted on later. I called those my choices, but a lot of them were just ways to keep the beast fed.

Freedom gives those ordinary decisions back. It doesn't always arrive with a big feeling. Sometimes it looks like an evening you remember and a morning you don't have to apologize for.

For me, it was football. I'd make chili and start drinking before kickoff. Pregame, in game, postgame. Football was my drinking pass, and I always knew when the game was on.

Now the pull is gone. I still enjoy a game, especially the people I watch it with, but some weekends I forget it's even on. It doesn't rule me anymore.

The drinking during the game was never the problem. It's that it didn't stop. Now the game is on low, the dog is at my feet, and there's a book in my lap. No pull. No debating a drink, or another drink, or whether I'll have enough to last through the night and morning until I can get to the liquor store. Freedom is driving by the liquor store and not even noticing it anymore. Freedom is not having to worry about which liquor store I went to yesterday in case the same guy was working.

Something true to hold onto: "It is for freedom that Christ has set us free. Stand firm, then, and do not let yourselves be burdened again by a yoke of slavery." (Galatians 5:1, NIV)

"So if the Son sets you free, you will be free indeed." (John 8:36, NIV)

Notice what comes first. Christ has set us free. Then comes stand firm. Not prove you're worth freeing. Not put on a convincing performance until God buys it. Start with what He's done, and live from there.

That matters when the old voice comes back. It knows your habits. It knows which hour used to belong to it. But knowing your old schedule doesn't make it your boss.

You don't have to spend every day arguing with alcohol. Alcohol is bananas. There's a whole life outside the argument, and some of it is waiting in the ordinary things you used to rush past.

A walk without calculating when you can get home and pour one. Breakfast without the sick feeling. Being present for the whole conversation. Nothing impressive to post. Just your life, with you actually in it.

If today still feels hard, you aren't pretending when you take one free step. A hard feeling doesn't cancel the step. Bring the uncertainty to God, and let your feet do one small thing the old master wouldn't have picked.

You don't need to look like a different man by dinner. Just stop handing the old one every decision.

Today's step: Do one ordinary thing today the free version of you would do: take a morning walk, eat a real breakfast, or spend twenty clear minutes on something you love. Write it in your Set Free note and check it off when it's done.
