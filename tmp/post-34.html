I used to think that even if I quit, I'd want a drink every day for the rest of my life. That was the part that scared me most. Not the first week. The idea of missing it forever, always one bad day from going back.

That's not what happened.

I can't explain it any other way than that God took it. Not dulled it. Took it. The thing I couldn't live without stopped coming up at all.

I had tried several times to quit drinking, and it never took. This time in my life was rock bottom. My marriage and finances were running on fumes. It may not sound like rock bottom, because I was on vacation in Mexico at an all-inclusive with unlimited booze. I was excited. A drinking pass for a week, with unlimited access 24 hours a day. I thought maybe I'd get lucky and just die on vacation.

God had other plans.

The first night, I stayed up and drank until 4 a.m. The second night, I didn't make it to dinner. I spent the next 24 hours dry heaving in the room, with terrible shakes the rest of the trip that I could not drink my way out of.

That was the last time I got drunk, but I still drank on the trip. I couldn't get drunk if I tried. It was surreal. I ended up calling my doctor's office back home from a beach in Mexico to schedule an appointment to talk about it.

When I got home, I tapered myself down over five nights before my doctor appointment. Five Michelob Ultras the first night, then four, then three, then two, then one. I didn't even want the last one. I drank half of it and never drank again. I'd had enough. I just didn't want it anymore.

I'm not telling you to do this. I'd read a lot about it and I know my body, but it's not a one size fits all plan. Talk to your doctor.

Looking back, God gave me a new heart and a new spirit in Mexico, in front of my wife, my best friends, and a couple of jolly ol' Englishmen we met on the trip.

Something true to hold onto: "I will give you a new heart and put a new spirit in you; I will remove from you your heart of stone and give you a heart of flesh." (Ezekiel 36:26, NIV)

"For sin shall no longer be your master, because you are not under the law, but under grace." (Romans 6:14, NIV)

A new heart is not something I can manufacture. I tried. I white-knuckled. I argued with myself for years. The peace didn't come when I finally won the war. It came when I stopped fighting it alone and handed the whole thing over.

I'm not telling you this to make it sound easy, and I'm not promising your story will look exactly like mine. Freedom is the point, not a scoreboard. But I need you to know the want is not the same thing as you. It can leave. Mine did. I still wake up surprised by that sometimes.
