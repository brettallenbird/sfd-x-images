Your life is your garden. If you don't like what grew, rip it out and plant something new.

I had to learn that the hard way. For a long time I stared at what the drinking years had grown and treated it like a life sentence. Like the garden was already decided, and I just had to live in the weeds.

It wasn’t.

When life gets hard, you have to change. When I first stopped, I just started taking walks with no phone. That was the first little habit I picked up after I quit drinking. A few weeks later, I changed up my diet. After that, I started lifting weights again. Nothing crazy, ten minutes. Then I started reading about positivity and confidence, which I had destroyed with my drinking. I always wanted to be an author, so I just started to write. That's where I am now. I wrote a book, and it's up for pre-order. Ask me two years ago and I would have thought you were crazy. Turns out, you can just do stuff. I'm getting addicted to executing ideas. Some will flop, some won't. It only takes one, at least that's what I'm told.

Something true to hold onto: "There is a time for everything, and a season for every activity under the heavens: a time to be born and a time to die, a time to plant and a time to uproot," (Ecclesiastes 3:1-2, NIV)

"Sow righteousness for yourselves, reap the fruit of unfailing love, and break up your unplowed ground; for it is time to seek the LORD, until he comes and showers his righteousness on you." (Hosea 10:12, NIV)

There's a time to uproot. That isn't cruelty. It's mercy. You don't have to keep watering what is killing you.

I'm not talking about ripping out people. I'm talking about the stuff you planted, or let get planted, that you never actually wanted. The nightly pour. The hidden bottles. The story that said this is just who I am. Rip that out. Then plant something else.

The beast wants you to believe the weeds are permanent. That it's too late to start over, that the soil is too far gone. But unplowed ground can be broken up. God says so. You seek Him, and He comes. You plant, and He showers.

What you plant now won't look like much at first. A clear morning. An honest sentence in your Set Free note. A walk instead of a pour. That's a seed. Give it time.

Today's step: In your Set Free note, write one thing growing in your life that you don't want anymore. Under it, write one new thing you'll plant this week in its place. One seed. Not the whole garden.
