Happy Thanksgiving.

Thanksgiving tables are full of people who still pour. For a long time, I needed a drink just to sit through dinner. The meal was something to get through so I could get back to the real plan.

Now the drink isn't a focus, it's all that food at the table that everyone so lovingly prepared. No hiding, no explaining. I'm there for the whole thing. The stories, the second plate, the people.

I would always get up and help with Thanksgiving. It was my wife's show and I was there to serve. But I always served myself first. I would be drinking while she was still getting ready hours before the party. Half in the bag when the guests arrived at 11am. I felt like hot garbage from the night before. I was drinking but I couldn't feel a buzz until I had 10 down plus pulls from a bottle hidden in the garage and then I would black out. Lord only knows what happened. I don't remember much.

I've told you about the night God asked me to count my blessings, and I realized I was wealthy. That still holds. Gratitude isn't a feeling I wait around for. It's a way of looking at what's already in front of me.

Something true to hold onto: "Give thanks to the LORD, for he is good; his love endures forever." (Psalm 107:1, NIV)

"Rejoice always, pray continually, give thanks in all circumstances; for this is God's will for you in Christ Jesus." (1 Thessalonians 5:16-18, NIV)

Look closely at that last one. It says give thanks in all circumstances, not for all circumstances. You don't have to be thankful for the hard year, the awkward relative, or the empty chair. You get to be thankful in the middle of it, because God is good and His love hasn't run out on you.

If today is hard, that's allowed too. Holidays stir things up. A few things that help me stay present:

  • Bring your own drink. Whatever you like that isn't alcohol. Have it in your hand before anyone offers. I like a travel mug or water bottle. No one asks or cares what is in it.
  • Be there to serve. Help in the kitchen, make sure you are the last to load up your plate, do the dishes. Come prepared with some jokes or stories to entertain your family, friends and everyone at the party.
  • Take a walk after dinner. Step outside for fifteen minutes. Fresh air resets a long day.
  • Leave when you're ready. You don't owe anyone the late shift.
  • Have a positive attitude and smile. People remember not what you said but how you make them feel.

Staying present is the real feast. You'll remember this one. All of it.

Today's step: Before you eat, write three things in your Set Free note that you're thankful for and that the bottle never gave you. If there's a moment at the table, say one of them out loud. If not, say it quietly to God.
