The invitations are starting. Christmas parties, family get-togethers, the neighbor's open house, the work thing. A lot of them come with a drink in the other hand.

When I first stopped, I felt like I needed everyone to be OK with it. I'd over-explain. I'd defend myself to people who hadn't even asked. I was ashamed that I had to stop, so I tried to win everybody's approval for it. That's exhausting, and it puts your freedom up for a vote it doesn't need.

It took me a while to learn that a short answer is enough. "No thanks, I'm good." If they press, "I don't drink anymore. Got tired of it." Then turn the conversation back to them. Most people are just making small talk. They'll move on faster than you will.

Something true to hold onto: "All you need to say is simply 'Yes' or 'No'; anything beyond this comes from the evil one." (Matthew 5:37, NIV)

"Fear of man will prove to be a snare, but whoever trusts in the LORD is kept safe." (Proverbs 29:25, NIV)

Jesus was talking about keeping your word, but there's freedom in it for us too. A plain yes or no is enough. You don't need to build a case. The long explanation usually isn't for them anyway. It's for the part of us that's still afraid of what they'll think.

That fear is a snare. It's how the beast gets back in the room. "Just have one so nobody makes it weird." But God sees you. He knows why you said no. That's the only approval you need.

The same goes for the invitations themselves. You can say no to a party. Not every event deserves your December. And you can say yes, go for an hour, and leave early. Find one person on your way out, say "Heading out, have a good one," and walk to your car.

I used to worry about all that stuff. Now it's being comfortable in my skin and body language. Shake my head, grab whatever drink I have wherever I set that down. "Nah" works fine. Look at them like you've matured out of it. When I see people drink now I feel like I am watching little kids doing foolish things.

You don't have to white-knuckle five weeks of parties. You just have to answer the next question in front of you. Yes or no, and remember, people are just making small talk. It might be at most 10 seconds of your day.
