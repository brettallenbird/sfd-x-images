Black Friday is a whole day built on one promise: the next thing will make you feel better.

I know that promise. Alcohol made it to me every single night. One more and you'll relax. One more and you'll feel like yourself. I paid for that promise over and over, and I burned through a whole retirement account chasing it. It never once delivered. Alcohol is bananas. You pay good money to feel worse.

There's a gap between where you are and where you want to be. The beast tells you to fill it with something you can pour. The world tells you to fill it with something you can buy. Both are selling the same thing.

I'm not against a good deal. Buy the gift. Get the thing you've been needing. Enjoy it. Just know what it can and can't do. A new TV can't fill the gap. Neither can a bottle.

Something else worth watching for. When you stop drinking, the itch sometimes looks for a new place to land. Spending. Scrolling. Something to fill the empty evening. That's not a failure. It's just worth noticing, so you can bring it to God instead of the checkout.

Something true to hold onto: "Why spend money on what is not bread, and your labor on what does not satisfy? Listen, listen to me, and eat what is good, and you will delight in the richest of fare." (Isaiah 55:2, NIV)

"Keep your lives free from the love of money and be content with what you have, because God has said, 'Never will I leave you; never will I forsake you.'" (Hebrews 13:5, NIV)

God asks the question I should have asked myself for years. Why spend on what doesn't satisfy? Then He doesn't scold. He invites. Listen. Eat what is good. Delight.

And contentment in Hebrews isn't built on having enough stuff. It's built on a promise: never will I leave you. That's the thing that fills the gap. Not more. Him.

These days the things I'm most grateful for didn't cost a thing. A clear morning. The dog at my feet. A Bible filling up with tabs. I couldn't have bought any of it, and the beast couldn't take it from me.

My favorite thing these days is my quiet mornings. I get up, walk, lift weights, read my Bible and work on my side project before the world needs my attention. It's such a gift seeing the sun come up every day.

Today's step: Before you buy anything today, ask yourself once: "Am I filling a need, or filling the gap?" Then in your Set Free note, write one thing you already have that no store could sell you.
