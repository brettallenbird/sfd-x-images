I set a sober timer the day I quit. It's still on my phone. These days I only check it before doctor appointments, because they ask sometimes. I don't feel like I am abstaining. Nobody else has ever asked me how many days.

That surprised me. Early on, I thought the number was the whole point.

I've told you about the six months I got through on pure grit. I counted every day back then. Every day was a little win I had to defend, and the bigger the number got, the heavier it felt. Then one vacation, and it was gone. All that counting, and the thing I was counting away from was still sitting right there, waiting.

That's what white-knuckling the calendar looks like. The want is still there. You're just stacking days on top of it and hoping the pile holds.

I wouldn't knock anyone for counting. If a number helps you get through a hard week, use it. Celebrate it. But if your sobriety depends on the count, I'd gently say you might be at risk. A count only looks backward. It tells you how long you've been holding on. It doesn't tell you whether anything inside you has changed.

This last time was different, and it wasn't because of the timer. I decided it was over, and God took the want. Once my heart changed, the days stopped being something I had to win. They just became my life.

The days don't set you free. A changed heart does. Until that changes, you're just white-knuckling the calendar.

It was one of those moments where you say to yourself, oh this is easy, now that I get it!

Something true to hold onto: "Brothers and sisters, I do not consider myself yet to have taken hold of it. But one thing I do: Forgetting what is behind and straining toward what is ahead, I press on toward the goal to win the prize for which God has called me heavenward in Christ Jesus." (Philippians 3:13-14, NIV)

"Teach us to number our days, that we may gain a heart of wisdom." (Psalm 90:12, NIV)

Paul had a past worth running from, and he didn't keep a ledger of how far he'd come. Forgetting what is behind. Straining toward what is ahead. And when the psalmist asks God to teach him to number his days, he isn't asking for a streak. He's asking for wisdom. Days aren't trophies. They're a gift you get to spend well, one at a time.

So if you count, that's fine. Toss the date in an app or a spreadsheet so you have it for your doctor. Then put your attention where it belongs: on today, and on what God is doing in your heart.

And if you lost a streak recently, hear this. Your heart didn't reset to zero. What God has been building in you didn't vanish because a number did. Keep knocking.
