Comedian Bill Burr has a line I keep coming back to. He's talking about the thoughts that pop into your head and drag you down, and he says, "All right, well, there's that thought. Do you want to have that thought or do you want to go to the gym?"

That's it. That's the whole trick, and it isn't really a trick.

A craving shows up as a thought. "Just one tonight." "You earned it." "Nobody would know." For years, I treated that thought like an order. It showed up, and I followed it to the fridge or the liquor store. I didn't know I had a choice in between.

The thought in my head in the morning would be no way I am drinking today. Then, I negotiated and thought, ok today is the last day. I had a lot of last days until it clicked and then I had the final day.

Here's what I wish I'd known. Having the thought isn't the same as acting on it. A thought isn't a relapse. It isn't proof you're broken. It's just the beast knocking on the door. You don't have to open it, and you don't have to stand there arguing through the door for an hour either.

Notice it. Name it. "There's that thought." Then go do the next thing. It's like birds flying up in the sky. You notice them, but don't let them nest on your head. After awhile you get used to the birds flying around sometimes and it turns to indifference.

Something true to hold onto: "Submit yourselves, then, to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you." (James 4:7, NIV)

"So I say, walk by the Spirit, and you will not gratify the desires of the flesh." (Galatians 5:16, NIV)

Notice the order again. Submit to God first. Then resist. You're not white-knuckling it alone. And Paul doesn't say sit still and fight the desire until it gives up. He says walk. Keep moving in the right direction, and the desire loses its grip.

That's why the gym line works. It's not about the gym. It's about having your next move picked before the thought ever shows up. For me, the next move was usually small. A walk with no phone. Ten minutes with the weights. Nothing crazy.

My days are full now. I am never bored, but back then I would go for a walk outside, do some calisthenics, or just go putz around in the yard or garage. Keeping the stress low.

The beast counts on you being caught off guard. It loves an empty hour and no plan. So take that away from it. Decide now what you'll do when the thought comes, and when it does, you won't have to decide anything. You'll just go.
